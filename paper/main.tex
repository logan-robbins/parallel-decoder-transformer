\begin{abstract}
Skeleton-of-Thought \citep{ning2024sot} established that a frozen LLM can decode
a partitioned answer in parallel branches with no inter-branch communication,
yielding up to $2.39\times$ lower latency. The system works; nobody has asked why.
We supply the missing measurement. On a frozen Qwen3-4B-Instruct across eight
fact-dense historical topics with $K{=}3$ chronologically partitioned streams, we
find that uncommunicating streams duplicate only $10.0\%$ of their fact atoms at
equal total token budget, and that the union recovers $1.05\times$ the atoms of a
sequential decode --- but that $71.8\%$ of those atoms are ones the sequential
decode never produced, so a sequential baseline is a \emph{different sample},
not a ceiling. We then probe the trunk's representation of the partition itself
and report a negative result with architectural consequences. Contrasting
segment-addressed against generic prompts yields vectors whose pairwise cosine
similarity separates ``first segment'' from the rest with depth
($\cos(v_0,v_1)$: $0.93 \to 0.68$) while $\cos(v_1,v_2)$ remains
$\approx 0.94$ at \emph{every} layer of 36: the trunk encodes a
\emph{skip-ahead axis}, not a $K$-way categorical address. Consistently, additive
activation steering along these directions performs at chance ($33\%$) while the
same partition stated in text succeeds ($89\%$). This falsifies the premise of
latent-bus architectures for parallel decoding, including our own prior proposal
\citep{robbins2025pdt}: a planner cannot deliver the address as an additive
latent, because the address is not linearly available at the residual stream.
Finally, we measure the hardware budget the whole approach rests on: per-step
decode latency is $1.08\times$ at $K{=}3$ but $1.57\times$ at $K{=}8$, so ``free''
parallelism expires near $K{\approx}4$. All experiments run in hours on one
consumer laptop; code and data are released.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Autoregressive decoding is sequential by construction: token $y_t$ conditions on
$y_{<t}$. A long-standing observation is that many answers are \emph{internally
parallel} --- an essay's sections, a timeline's eras, a list's items --- so a
model could in principle write them at once. \citet{ning2024sot} turned this into
a working system: prompt the model for a skeleton, expand each skeleton point in
a separate batched branch, concatenate. No branch sees any other. They report
$>2\times$ speedup on 8 of 12 models.

This raises a question the original work did not ask: \emph{why does it work?}
Concatenating text written by mutually blind writers should produce
contradictions, repetition, and gaps. That it does not is a fact about the model
and the task, and it has never been measured. The follow-on question is
architectural. Several proposals --- including one of our own
\citep{robbins2025pdt} --- argue that the absence of inter-branch communication is
a defect to be repaired with a learned latent channel, and that a planner should
seed each stream with a latent plan rather than a text prompt. Both halves of
that argument are empirical claims that nobody has tested.

We test them. Our contributions are measurements, not mechanisms; the mechanism
is \citeauthor{ning2024sot}'s.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Cross-stream redundancy is small} (\S\ref{sec:crowding}). At equal
total token budget, $K{=}3$ uncommunicating streams duplicate $10.0\%$ of fact
atoms. The partition, not communication, does the work. This is the quantity a
coordination bus would exist to reclaim, and it is the first time it has been
put on a scale.

\item \textbf{A sequential baseline is not a ceiling} (\S\ref{sec:crowding}).
Parallel streams recover only $30.2\%$ of sequential's atoms, but produce
$1.05\times$ as many atoms overall, of which $71.8\%$ are novel. Budget-matched
sequential decoding is a broad shallow survey; $K$ streams at $B/K$ each are deep
dives. Recall-against-sequential --- the obvious metric --- is therefore
misleading, and we report why.

\item \textbf{The trunk has a skip-ahead axis, not a $K$-way address}
(\S\ref{sec:geometry}). This is our main result, and it is negative for latent
planners. We support it against three attacks: rogue dimensions
\citep{timkey2021rogue}, contrast-baseline artifacts, and the possibility that a
linear probe succeeds where a mean-difference direction fails.

\item \textbf{Free parallelism expires at $K{\approx}4$} (\S\ref{sec:latency}).
Per-step decode latency is $1.08\times$ at $K{=}3$ and $1.57\times$ at $K{=}8$ on
our hardware. The memory-bound regime that motivates the entire approach has a
budget.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What we do not claim.} We do not claim the architecture, which is
\citet{ning2024sot}'s. We do not claim the speedup: our $2.79\times$ at $K{=}3$
sits inside their reported range, and their strongest category (``knowledge'',
$2.33\times$) is precisely our regime. We do not claim equal-budget evaluation as
an innovation: their Appendix I.3 already compares at matched length and reaches
the same conclusion. Our budget control is exact rather than prompt-nudged, which
is rigor, not insight.

\section{Setup}

\paragraph{Model.} Frozen Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 \citep{qwen3}, 36 layers, hidden
size 2560, \texttt{float32} on Apple M4 (MPS), greedy decoding throughout. We use
the instruction-tuned rather than base variant deliberately: a frozen trunk can
only be steered toward behavior it already exhibits, so a variant that does not
follow the text form of an instruction cannot serve as a control for whether the
latent form works.

\paragraph{Task.} Eight fact-dense historical topics with objective chronological
boundaries (Second World War, American Civil War, Apollo program, French
Revolution, Napoleon, Cold War, powered flight, Manhattan Project). Each is
partitioned into $K{=}3$ contiguous date ranges. Chronology is not incidental:
it makes the partition both externally checkable and cheap to name.

\paragraph{Addresses are minimal.} A stream's entire plan is a date range
(``covering only 1942 to 1943''): $\log_2 3 = 1.58$ bits to select among
segments. An earlier design used rich addresses (``from the invasion of Poland in
1939 through Pearl Harbor'') and we discarded it: such an address \emph{hands the
stream the very atoms it is then scored on}, which the sequential arm never
receives, and it would put $H(\text{partition} \mid x)$ at 40+ bits rather than 2.

\paragraph{Fact atoms.} Years, numbers, and multiword proper nouns, extracted
lexically by one shared extractor applied identically to both arms. Atoms
occurring in \emph{any} prompt --- including the boundary years and the topic
name --- are excluded from scoring on both sides, so neither arm is credited for
facts it was given. The extractor is deliberately crude and deliberately
symmetric; it cannot favor either arm.

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Prompt-partitioned parallel decoding.} \citet{ning2024sot} is the
direct antecedent of this work and of the systems we critique: a frozen LLM emits
a skeleton, branches expand points in parallel with no communication, outputs are
concatenated; $1.13$--$2.39\times$ across 12 LLMs, with a router deciding when to
apply it. \citet{apar2024} fine-tunes models to emit fork control tokens so
decoding jumps ahead within one sequence. \citet{pasta2025} learns an annotation
language in which the model itself marks semantic independence, and reports
realized and theoretical speedups. Our system is \citeauthor{ning2024sot}'s with
the planner hand-specified; our contribution is measurement inside it.

\paragraph{Communicating parallel workers.} \citet{hogwild2025} runs concurrent
workers over a shared, RoPE-rotated KV cache so every worker attends to every
other's in-progress tokens --- the maximal-bandwidth counterpart to SoT's zero.
\citet{groupthink2025} has $K$ concurrent thinkers with token-level visibility
into each other's partial generations. \citet{robbins2025pdt} proposes a narrow
learned latent bus with planner seeding, and is the proposal our
\S\ref{sec:geometry} result argues against.

\paragraph{Speculative and diffusion decoding.} Speculative decoding
\citep{leviathan2023spec}, Medusa \citep{cai2024medusa}, and EAGLE
\citep{li2024eagle} accelerate decoding while \emph{preserving the output
distribution exactly}. Diffusion LMs \citep{fastdllm2025,tidar2025} parallelize
token positions within one sequence. Both differ from this line in kind: we
produce different text, not the same text faster. \citet{bitstorounds2025}
establishes a bits-to-rounds principle for diffusion LMs --- decoding rounds grow
with total information and inversely with per-round budget --- which is
information-theoretic but concerns positions within one sequence, not
coordination across streams.

\paragraph{Representation geometry and steering.} Activation addition
\citep{turner2023actadd}, task vectors \citep{ilharco2023task}, and function
vectors \citep{todd2024function} establish that behaviors can be steered by
adding directions to the residual stream. \citet{park2025geometry} shows
categorical concepts with $k$ values form simplices --- but in \emph{unembedding}
space, explicitly declining to address internal structure. \citet{tan2024steering}
documents steering-vector unreliability across inputs.
\citet{heyman2026steerlike} shows activation steering is not faithful to
prompting's mechanics: prompting intervenes strongly on some tokens and barely on
others. Our \S\ref{sec:geometry} is a specific instance with an architectural
consequence, and \citet{timkey2021rogue} supplies the null hypothesis we test
against. \citet{dong2025emergent} probes prompt representations for attributes of
the not-yet-generated response, which is the readout site we use.

\section{Limitations}

One model, one size, one language, one task family, one machine. The fact-atom
extractor is lexical and cannot see paraphrase, so two streams stating the same
fact in different words register as no overlap --- our $10.0\%$ crowding is
therefore a \emph{lower bound}. Chronological partitions are the easy case by
construction; \citet{ning2024sot} already identified writing, math, and coding as
the regimes where decomposition fails, and we did not test them. Our geometry
result concerns one readout site (last prompt token) and one intervention family
(additive, all positions); it establishes that the address is not \emph{there},
not that no latent encoding exists.
